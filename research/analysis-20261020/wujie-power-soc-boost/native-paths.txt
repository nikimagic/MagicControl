MAIN FINDINGS
1. There are separate numeric envelopes (SPL/SPPT/FPPT) and separate firmware policy flags. None of the inspected three Overboost/HighPower setters contains a duration/tau, SOC comparison or delayed reset.
2. An additional native bit exists: 0x7A6.1 (mask02) is inverted dynamic-temperature Overboost admission, not a power timer: enabled->clear, disabled->set. The setters for 7A5.7, 7A5.4 and 7A6.1 have no call sites in the inspected RAMFan1.5 class; the legacy Intel/QC managers do call them.
3. No exact SOC percentage threshold or short/long Turbo duration was found in this Wujie-relevant host code. Owner observation of lower-SOC EC limiting is credible input but remains a firmware behavior to correlate; it is not explained or disproved by absence of a C# comparison.

TIME / SOC CONCLUSIONS AND GAPS
- No exact 'SOC X% -> lower budget' threshold/hysteresis/delay was found in this reference host call chain. SOC-dependent EC firmware policy remains unknown, not contradicted.
- No verified duration in seconds for short Turbo or long Turbo. Distinct Fast/Slow/STAPM values are measurable; time constants and EC thermal/SOC arbitration are not established by names.
- CPU.cs has AmdSPL/SPPT/FPPT values but no corresponding AMD time-window fields in this reference model. Stopwatch elapsedMilliseconds around FAN/profile async operations (RAMFan1.5:5764-5802 etc.) measure operation latency, not power boost duration.
- 7C3 can change firmware budgets depending on supply rating and likely other firmware inputs; SOC effect cannot be reduced to platform4/7 without controlled evidence.
- No new hardware experiment is authorized by this task. If future owner asks, evidence should correlate actual SOC, AC/Type-C rating,751,7C3,7A5,7A6, overrides, actual SMU Fast/Slow/STAPM and measured power over time while preserving FAN ownership. Exact EC firmware consumer/thermal timing remains the source-level evidence needed for absolute thresholds; raw ec.bin is a RAM snapshot, not that executable.
